Bulb Brightness

Predict which bulbs glow brighter in series and parallel circuits.

Resistor Circuits

Find equivalent resistances, currents and voltages step by step, and measure them with ammeters and voltmeters.

Potential in a Circuit

Follow the potential around a circuit: up across a battery, down across each lamp, flat along wires; the loop and junction rules, voltages between points, and the wrong step in a sketch.

Capacitors and RC Circuits

Capacitors in series and in parallel (the rules of resistors, swapped), voltage and current while a capacitor charges and discharges, and the time constant and half-life read from a graph.

Impedance Curves

Sketch the impedance of R, L and C against frequency, predict from ωL and 1/(ωC) what a circuit does at low and high frequencies, and spot resonance as the minimum (series) or maximum (parallel) of Z.

Switching RL Circuits

Right after a switch flips in a circuit with coils: the inductor current cannot jump.

Torque and Equilibrium

Lever arms and torques with their sense of rotation, the weight at the centre of mass, and both conditions of equilibrium, including the force at the pivot.

Centre of Mass and Tipping

Centres of mass of wire figures, stable, unstable and neutral equilibrium, and when a tilted body falls over.

Point Charges

The Coulomb force: which way it points, by what factor it changes with the charges and the distance, forces and fields of several charges added as arrows, and the field as force per charge, E = F/q.

Energy Conservation

Energy bookkeeping: the forms of energy in each state as bar charts, the energy balance solved with symbols, springs, friction, and why the turning point of a bungee jump is not its equilibrium.

Force and Motion

Inertia, the net force and acceleration, balanced forces in motion and the force towards the centre on a circle: spot the misconceptions behind tempting answers.

Interaction

Newton’s third law: equal and opposite forces on different bodies, finding the partner of a force, and why weight and normal force are no pair.

Free-Body Diagrams

Draw every force on a chosen system, including spring forces and air resistance, resolve the weight on a slope, choose the system that makes a rope force external, and set up Newton’s second law along each axis.

Electromagnetic Induction

From a flux graph to the voltage it induces and back, a loop moving through a field, Lenz's rule: the slope counts, not the value.

Motion Data

Positions and distances, instants and intervals: value tables, stroboscope pictures and s(t) graphs, uniform or uniformly accelerated.

Slopes of Motion Graphs

Velocity as the slope of s(t), negative velocities, and sketching v(t) from s(t) and a(t) from v(t).

Areas under Motion Graphs

Displacement as the area under v(t), negative below the axis: who is farther, mean velocity, and sketching s(t) from v(t) and v(t) from a(t).

Oscillations

Simple harmonic motion as the shadow of a turning pointer: harmonic or not, equations and graphs, where v and a are largest, and the LC circuit as the same equation.

Cyclic Processes

Processes of an ideal gas in p(V), p(T) and V(T) diagrams: describe, draw, read and switch between them.

Wave Propagation

Wave crests on a rope: how they travel, how a point of the rope moves, how they overlap, how they are reflected at a fixed or free end, and the standing waves they form.

Interference and Diffraction

Huygens’ wavelets behind a gap, bright fringes from the path difference at a double slit and a grating, how the pattern changes with slit width, spacing and wavelength, and the resolving power.

Field Lines and Equipotentials

Sketching and reading the field lines of point charges, a wire and a capacitor, dipoles in a uniform field, equipotential lines, and finding the error in a sketch.

Electric Potential

Potential, potential energy and voltage told apart, ΔV = E·d in a uniform field, the potential of point charges by sign and distance, and whether a charge gains or loses potential energy.

Magnetic Forces and Fields of Currents

Field lines of wires, loops, solenoids and magnets with the grip rule, the hand rules for charges and currents, the force F = I·L·B·sin θ, attracting and repelling currents, and the torque on a coil in motors, loudspeakers and meters.

Charged Particles in Fields

Energy from an accelerating voltage in electronvolts, the parabola in a capacitor and the circle in a magnetic field, why a magnetic field never changes the speed, radius and period by ratios, the velocity selector and the Hall voltage.

Electromagnetic Waves

Order the spectrum by wavelength, frequency and photon energy, follow light into matter (c/n, same frequency, shorter wave), and find the direction of travel from E and B.

Photoelectric Effect

Light in portions: what the wave model cannot explain, photon energy in electronvolts, Einstein’s equation as energy bookkeeping with the stopping voltage, the characteristic of a photocell and the Vs(f) graph.

Matter Waves and the Particle in a Box

De Broglie wavelengths by ratios, electron diffraction as evidence for matter waves, the standing waves and quantised energies of a particle in a box, and |ψ|² with its expectation value and uncertainty.
