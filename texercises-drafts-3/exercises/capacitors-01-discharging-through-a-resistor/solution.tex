\begin{abcliste}
\abc The time constant and the current right after the switch is closed are
\begin{align}
 \tau &= \tcF = \Res\cdot\Cap = \tc \approx \result{\tcP{0}{2}} \\
 I_0 &= \IoF = \frac{\Uo}{\Res} = \Io \approx \result{\IoP{-3}{2}}
\end{align}
\begin{itemize}
\item $\Delta V_C$: the charge on the plates cannot vanish at once, so the voltage does not jump. It starts at $\Delta V_0 = \UoO$ and decays exponentially, $\Delta V_C = \Delta V_0\,e^{-t/\tau}$: steepest at the start, down to 37\,\% after $\tau$, almost zero after $5\tau$.
\item $\Delta V_R$: the resistor is connected straight across the capacitor, so $\Delta V_R = \Delta V_C$ at every moment once the switch is closed. Before $t = 0$ it is zero; at $t = 0$ it jumps to $\Delta V_0$ and then follows the same curve.
\item $I = \Delta V_R/R$: zero before $t = 0$, a jump to $I_0$, then the same exponential decay.
\end{itemize}

(Through the resistor the current flows the other way than while the capacitor was being charged.)

\abc The resistor is connected straight across the capacitor, so
\begin{align}
 \Delta V_R &= \Delta V_C = \result{\UaO}
\end{align}
and the current is
\begin{align}
 I &= \IaF = \frac{\Ua}{\Res} = \Ia \approx \result{\IaP{-3}{2}}
\end{align}

\abc There is no battery in the circuit: the capacitor itself drives the current, and the only loop is capacitor and resistor. The loop rule gives $\Delta V_R = \Delta V_C$. The rule $\Delta V_R = \Delta V_0 - \Delta V_C$ (here $\UwP{0}{2}$) belongs to charging, where a battery with $\Delta V_0$ is in the loop.

$I = \Delta V_0/R = \IoP{-3}{2}$ is the current at $t = 0$ only. As the charge flows off, $\Delta V_C$ and with it the current decrease: at this moment the current is $\Delta V_C/R$.

\abc From $\Delta V_C = \Delta V_0\,e^{-t/\tau}$:
\begin{align}
 t &= \taF = \Res\cdot\Cap\cdot\ln\frac{\Uo}{\Ua} = \ta \approx \result{\taP{0}{2}}
\end{align}
That is about $1.3\,\tau$.
