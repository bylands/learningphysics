\begin{abcliste}
\abc The lines start on $+q$ and end on $-q$, and the arrows point from $+$ to $-$: that is right. But \result{\text{the field lines cross}}. The student drew the radial lines of each charge on its own, as if the other charge were not there.

\abc The field at a point is the sum of the fields of both charges: it has only one direction. A field line runs along this direction. If two lines crossed, the field at the crossing point would have two directions at once.

\abc The lines leave $+q$, bend round and end on $-q$; the lines between the charges are densest. Lines leaving $+q$ on its far side make wide arcs or leave the picture, and come back to $-q$ from outside.
