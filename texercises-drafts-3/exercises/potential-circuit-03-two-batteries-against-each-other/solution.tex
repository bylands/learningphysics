\abc The sketch ends at \SI{8}{\V} back at A instead of \SI{0}{\V}, so one step is wrong. A\,$\to$\,B (a rise of \SI{12}{\V}), C\,$\to$\,D (a drop of \SI{5}{\V} across $L_1$), E\,$\to$\,F (a drop of \SI{3}{\V} across $L_2$) and the flat wires are right.

The wrong step is \result{\text{D}\to\text{E}}: the current flows through the \SI{4}{\V} battery from $+$ to $-$, so the potential drops there by \SI{4}{\V}; the sketch rises by \SI{4}{\V}.

\abc A battery always keeps its $+$ terminal \SI{4}{\V} above its $-$ terminal, whichever way the current flows. Going from D to E with the current, we go from the $+$ terminal to the $-$ terminal: down by \SI{4}{\V}. The stronger \SI{12}{\V} battery drives the current backwards through the \SI{4}{\V} battery, which then takes energy from the charges, like a lamp. (A rise across a battery belongs to going through it from $-$ to $+$, as across the \SI{12}{\V} battery from A to B.)
