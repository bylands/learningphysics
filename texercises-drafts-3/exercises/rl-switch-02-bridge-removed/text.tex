A battery with $V = \VbO$ feeds a resistor $R_1 = \RaO$ and then two parallel branches: a resistor $R_2 = \RbO$ in series with an inductor $L = \LcO$, and a resistor $R_3 = \RcO$. A switch S across $R_1$ bridges it while it is closed. The battery and the inductor have no resistance of their own. The arrows show the positive direction of the currents; $I$ is the current through the battery.

\begin{center}
\begin{circuitikz}[scale=0.9, transform shape]
 \draw (0,0) to[battery1, invert, l=$V$] (0,4) to[short, i>^=$I$] (0.8,4) to[R, l_=$R_1$] (3,4) -- (6.5,4);
 \node at (0.4,2.25) {$+$}; \node at (0.4,1.75) {$-$};
 \draw (0.8,4) -- (0.8,5.1) -- (1.5,5.1);
 \draw (1.5,5.1) node[ocirc] {} -- (2.3,5.1) node[ocirc] {};
 \node[above] at (1.9,5.15) {S};
 \draw (2.3,5.1) -- (3,5.1) -- (3,4);
 \draw (4,4) to[R, l_=$R_2$, i>^=$I_2$] (4,2) to[L, l_=$L$] (4,0);
 \draw (6.5,4) to[R, l=$R_3$, i>_=$I_3$] (6.5,0) -- (0,0);
 \fill (0.8,4) circle (2pt) (3,4) circle (2pt) (4,4) circle (2pt) (4,0) circle (2pt);
\end{circuitikz}
\end{center}

\begin{abcliste}
\abc The switch S has been closed for a long time. Find the currents $I$, $I_2$ and $I_3$ and the current through $R_1$.
\abc At $t = 0$ the switch is opened. Find the currents $I$, $I_2$ and $I_3$ a long time afterwards.
\abc Why does the inductance $L$ play no part in (a) and (b)?
\end{abcliste}
