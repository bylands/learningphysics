\begin{abcliste}
\abc The sketch ends at \SI{6}{\V} back at A, not at \SI{0}{\V} where it started. A point has only one potential: around a closed loop the rises must add up to the drops (loop rule). Here they do not, so one of the steps is wrong.

\abc Check the steps one at a time. From A to B the potential rises by \SI{9}{\V} across the battery (from $-$ to $+$): right. Along the wires B\,$\to$\,C, D\,$\to$\,E and F\,$\to$\,A it stays the same: right. Across $L_1$ (C\,$\to$\,D) it drops by \SI{6}{\V} in the direction of the current: right.

The wrong step is \result{\text{E}\to\text{F}}: across the lamp $L_2$ the potential drops by \SI{3}{\V} in the direction of the current; the sketch rises by \SI{3}{\V} there. A lamp takes energy from the charges, so the potential cannot rise across it.

\abc The right potentials:
