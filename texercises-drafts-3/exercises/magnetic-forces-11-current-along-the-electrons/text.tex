In a straight wire the electrons drift to the left. The wire lies in a magnetic field that points up the page.

\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[>=Stealth]
 \foreach \x in {0.3,1.3,...,5.3} \draw[->, gray, thick] (\x,-1.3) -- (\x,1.3);
 \node[gray, above] at (5.3,1.3) {$\vec B$};
 \fill[gray!25] (-0.3,-0.2) rectangle (6,0.2);
 \draw (-0.3,-0.2) -- (6,-0.2) (-0.3,0.2) -- (6,0.2);
 \foreach \x in {1.8,3.8} {
  \fill[blue!60!black] (\x,0) circle (0.15); \node[white] at (\x,0) {\tiny $-$};
  \draw[->, very thick] (\x-0.2,0.45) -- (\x-1.0,0.45);}
 \node[above] at (1.4,0.45) {$\vec v_\mathrm{d}$};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

Luca finds the direction of the magnetic force on the wire in four steps:
\begin{itemize}
\item[1.] The electrons move to the left, so the current flows to the left too.
\item[2.] For a current, the right hand: thumb along the current, to the left.
\item[3.] Index finger along the field, up the page.
\item[4.] The middle finger points into the page: that is the force.
\end{itemize}

\begin{abcliste}
\abc One step is wrong. Which one, and why?
\abc In which direction does the magnetic force on the wire point?
\end{abcliste}
