\begin{abcliste}
\abc The field lines are right: they leave the charges, never cross and bend away from the middle between the charges. But \result{\text{the equipotential lines cross}}: the student drew circles round each charge on its own, as if the other charge were not there.

\abc At each point the potential has only one value, the sum of the potentials of the two charges, $V = k\,\frac{q}{r_1} + k\,\frac{q}{r_2}$. At a crossing point it would have the two different values of the two lines. Besides, an equipotential crosses the field lines at right angles; the circles of the sketch do not, away from the charges.

\abc Close to each charge its own potential wins: small closed loops round each charge. Further out, the two charges look more and more like one charge $2q$: the equipotentials become single ovals round both, and far away almost circles. In between, the line through the midpoint between the charges has the shape of a figure eight. Everywhere the equipotentials cross the field lines at right angles.
