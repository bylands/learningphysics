\begin{abcliste}
\abc
\begin{itemize}
\item The lines start on the positive charge and end on the negative plate. Because the plate carries a net negative charge, more lines end on it than the charge sends out: further lines come down from far above and end on the plate too.
\item A metal plate is an equipotential: the field lines meet its surface at right angles, also right next to the charge.
\item The negative charge on the plate is pulled towards $+q$ and gathers below it: there the lines are densest.
\item The fields of the plate and of the charge add up to one field, so the lines never cross. Above the charge the upward field of $+q$ and the downward field of the plate cancel at one point: the lines leaving $+q$ upwards bend round and go down to the plate as well.
\end{itemize}

\abc The right diagram is \result{(3)}.
\begin{itemize}
\item (1): the lines end on the charge, as if it were negative (the field of $-q$ above the plate). Field lines start on positive charges.
\item (2): the lines are right, but the arrows point the wrong way: field lines run from $+$ to $-$, away from $+q$ and into the negative plate.
\item (4): the field of the charge and the field of the plate are drawn each on their own, so the lines cross, and the radial lines meet the metal at an angle. At each point the field has only one direction, that of the sum of the two fields.
\end{itemize}
\end{abcliste}
